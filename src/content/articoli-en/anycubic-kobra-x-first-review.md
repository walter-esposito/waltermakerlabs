The Kobra X has arrived. Unboxing, full assembly, setup and first prints, through to the results after **14 days** of use.

## Assembly and setup

The machine arrives partly assembled: base and gantry separate. The stages are the familiar ones &mdash; fixing the gantry to the base, covers and belts, connecting the cabling &mdash; plus two things specific to this machine: the **purge cutting system** and the spool holders with their PTFE tubes.

The purge cutting system deserves a note. On a single-filament machine with automatic changing, purges have to be handled physically, because the material ejected at every colour change has to go somewhere. That's an extra mechanical assembly that simply doesn't exist on a single-colour printer.

After assembly: first boot, Wi-Fi setup, the Anycubic app, calibration and a firmware update. Filament loading uses **RFID** recognition of the spools.

## The first prints

The first test is a **Speed Benchy**, the most widely used reference for judging a machine at high speed: a known model whose overhangs and detail immediately expose cooling and acceleration faults.

After the benchy, two checks that matter more than any single print:

- **A bed flatness test**, because a plate that isn't true shows up as a first-layer problem no profile will fix
- **Results after 14 days**, which is the only way to tell a machine that prints well from a machine that prints well on day one

## The materials tested

PLA, PETG, TPU and ASA. That's a sensible spread because it covers four different behaviours: PLA the easy one, PETG needing more care with adhesion, flexible TPU stressing the filament path, and ASA which shrinks and wants stable temperatures.

## Footprint

Among the measurements reported is the machine's real footprint — a figure almost nobody publishes, and one you need if you're working out whether it fits on the bench. Build volume and external dimensions are not proportional: an enclosed machine takes up far more space than its usable volume suggests.
