Eight print heads, a heated chamber and a 300 &times; 300 &times; 300 mm build volume, for **$499**. For comparison: a $899 Snapmaker U1 has four.

The community reaction was unanimous — it looks too good to be true. So it's worth going through what has actually been published, what's missing, and which technical questions decide whether the machine works or not.

> **Disclosure:** I don't own this printer, I haven't printed on it, and I'm not sponsored by InfiMech. This is a technical preview based on public information, not a review.

## The two machines

| | InfiMech MX | InfiMech MX Pro |
|---|---|---|
| VIP price | $249 | $499 |
| List price | $399 | **$1,399** (was $899 in July) |
| Toolheads | 6 | 8 |
| Build volume | 220 &times; 220 &times; 220 mm | 300 &times; 300 &times; 300 mm |
| Frame | open | enclosed, actively heated chamber |
| Nozzle | &mdash; | up to 300 &deg;C |
| Extras | &mdash; | closed-loop motors, AI monitoring, air filtration |

Shared by both: induction heating, NFC telemetry, adaptive offset calibration, a nozzle sensor for blob detection, tangled-filament detection, power-loss recovery, automatic levelling and a tilting 5" touchscreen.

## The figure you won't find on the official pages

During the 13 August livestream, an InfiMech engineer gave the number that appears nowhere in the official material: **a tool change takes around 15 seconds.**

That matters, and it needs context. Fifteen seconds is three times the five seconds claimed by other toolchangers. On a print with two hundred changes, that's a fifty-minute difference. It isn't disqualifying, but at $499 it's exactly the kind of trade-off you'd expect to find somewhere.

## Watch the pricing: what the deposit actually guarantees

**Update, 12 August:** InfiMech's new FAQ clarifies that the $5 deposit does **not** guarantee the $249/$499 price. You need a Super Early Bird slot, and those are capped.

Meanwhile the MX Pro list price went **from $899 to $1,399**. Anyone who doesn't get a slot isn't paying $499 — they're paying $1,399.

This is precisely the mechanism to be clear-eyed about before putting any money down.

## Some good news: the machine exists

A classic hardware-crowdfunding risk is funding a render. Not here:

- it printed live at the **Japan RepRap Festival** in May
- it runs daily in the company's **Yiwu store**

The "project doesn't exist" risk is off the table. The others remain.

## The issue nobody likes to raise

There is a **patent** question hanging over the Bondtech and INDX approaches. On a product landing at prices this low, it's the kind of problem that doesn't surface during a campaign but can materialise afterwards, once production is running.

<p class="avviso-affiliato">
<strong>A word on crowdfunding:</strong> backing a campaign means funding a product into existence,
not buying it. Pledges are charged when the campaign closes regardless of progress, and recovering
money from failed projects is rare. Only commit amounts you're comfortable seeing arrive late, or
not arrive at all.
