## The prices, and what you actually pay

| | MX (6 toolheads) | MX Pro (8 toolheads) |
|---|---|---|
| Super Early Bird at launch | $299 | $549 |
| With the $50 VIP cashback | $249 | $499 |
| List price | $399 | $1,399 |

The campaign page puts it this way: VIP "Pay $5 for VIP Access, Get $50 Cashback", Super Early Bird
"No deposit required". So you can claim a Super Early Bird slot without a deposit.

The $249 and $499 figures that have been around since August **aren't a price you see at checkout**.
The $5 VIP deposit buys a **$50 credit**, and the campaign page now says when it comes back: **after
your printer has shipped**, to your original payment method. So you pay $299 or $549 at checkout, and
the $50 returns when the machine ships, months later. Each VIP reservation covers one printer: two VIPs
and two printers, $100 back.

**Super Early Bird slots were limited.** Now that the campaign is live, check the rewards tab for
which tier is still available: if you're late, you pay the next tier up.

### How to decide without risk

On Kickstarter **you don't pay when you pledge**: your card is charged only when the campaign closes,
and until then you can **lower or cancel** your pledge. The one limit is the final 24 hours: then
Kickstarter won't let you cancel if pulling out would drop the project below its goal.

So until **9 December** you can reserve the tier you want and decide as information comes in: official
updates, answers in the comments, reports from Formnext. If something doesn't convince you, cancel
before the final 24 hours and you've paid nothing. With the goal exceeded this far, the final-24-hours
limit won't realistically apply, but don't count on it: decide earlier.

Two things to read carefully. Mass production is planned **one month after** the campaign opens:
that's a tight schedule for a project that has already moved its launch three times. And "first
batch" doesn't mean every backer: if you join later in the campaign, your machine may come later.

## Warranty and support

| Coverage | Length |
|---|---|
| Structural, mechanical and electronic components | 12 months, **2 years where legally required (EU and UK)** |
| VIP reservation holders | 2-year full warranty on core components |
| Wear parts (hotend assembly, extruder gear, PTFE tube) | 3 months |

The page also lists a "6-month limited warranty" without saying what it covers: worth asking InfiMech
during the campaign.

What matters most is the **service method**: "Official replacement parts shipped directly to users",
meaning **they send you the part and you fit it**. There's no repair centre to send the printer to.
On a toolchanger with six or eight heads, that means sooner or later you'll be taking a head apart
yourself.

## The add-ons

During the campaign you can add to your pledge: **spare toolhead, build plate, feeder
module, filament, 10 W laser module, camera and side-blower fan**.

The prices below are the ones published before launch, labelled **estimates subject to change**: the
add-ons tab on Kickstarter is what counts.

What I notice:

- **Toolheads are cheap: $45 a pair**, about $22.50 each, with your choice of nozzle size. On a
  toolchanger that's the spare part that matters most, and the price is low.
- **Camera and side-blower fan are add-ons for the MX only**, because they're standard on the Pro. An
  MX with both costs $73 more: $372 at the Super Early Bird price, before shipping.
- **The laser is Pro only**, at $129. It isn't available for the MX.
- The filament bundles are sized to the machine: **6 spools for the MX, 8 for the Pro**. The colours
  **are fixed and can't be chosen**: PLA-HF ×6 is black, white, red, grey, yellow and orange; ×8 adds
  pine green and blue. PETG-HF ×6 is black, white, grey, orange, red and gold; ×8 adds green and
  silver. The TPU-95A ×2 + PLA-HF ×2 kit is orange and white TPU plus white and pine green PLA; the
  PA6-CF is black.
- **MX and MX Pro toolheads are not interchangeable**: pick the version for your machine.
- **One feeder module is enough for an MX Pro**: InfiMech confirmed it to me, and the page says so
  too. It covers all 8 filament channels and dry boxes.
- **Watch the nozzle sizes.** According to an InfiMech reply quoted in the campaign comments, **mixing
  nozzle diameters in a single print isn't supported** right now: to print everything at 0.6 mm you
  need 0.6 on every toolhead, so several spare pairs, not one.

### The laser module

I asked InfiMech for the laser details (it's MX Pro only). Their answer:

- **semiconductor diode, 445 ± 5 nm wavelength**, i.e. blue light;
- engraves and cuts **wood, non-transparent acrylic, leather, paper, oxide-coated metal, leaves,
  non-mirror stainless steel, stone, mugs**;
- **8 mm plywood cut at 120 mm/min**;
- it **docks like a toolhead**, using the same tool-changing structure.

The usual blue-laser limit applies: it won't cut **clear acrylic**, and bare metal only marks if it has
an oxide layer. 120 mm/min through 8 mm plywood is slow cutting: fine for the odd part, not for
production runs.

## Full spec sheet: MX vs MX Pro

This is the table published on the campaign page. InfiMech calls it **provisional and subject to
change** until the machine ships.

| | MX Pro | MX |
|---|---|---|
| External dimensions | 453 × 488 × 563 mm | 361 × 401 × 402 mm |
| Build volume | 300 × 300 × 300 mm | 220 × 220 × 220 mm |
| Weight | 26 kg | 13 kg |
| Frame | Steel, enclosed | Steel, open |
| Toolheads | 8 | 6 |
| Max nozzle temperature | 350 °C | 310 °C |
| Motors | **Closed-loop** | Open-loop |
| Max speed | 800 mm/s | 600 mm/s |
| Max acceleration | 20,000 mm/s² | 20,000 mm/s² |
| Bed | 110 °C, textured plate | 110 °C, textured plate |
| Heated chamber | 60–65 °C | No |
| Air filter | HEPA and activated carbon | No |
| Camera | 1080p 30 fps, standard | Optional |
| AI monitoring | Yes | No |
| Side-blower fan | Standard | Optional |
| Laser module | Optional | Not available |
| Max power | 1,850 W at 220 V (1,300 W at 110 V) | 1,150 W at 220 V (700 W at 110 V) |

**Shared:** HRC50+ hardened steel nozzles, induction heating, wireless thermistor, 1.75 mm filament,
adaptive calibration, blob, run-out and tangle detection, power-loss recovery, plate detection, 5"
screen (480 × 854), 8 GB storage plus microSD, OrcaSlicer or InfiMech Orca, operating temperature
10–30 °C.

**Materials.** The Pro prints PLA, PETG, ABS, ASA, TPU, PET, PA and PC, plus carbon- or glass-filled
grades (PLA, PETG, ABS, ASA, PA, PA6, PA12, PAHT, PPA, PET). The MX is meant for PLA, PETG, TPU, PVA,
PLA-CF and PETG-CF; ABS, ASA, PA, PC and PET are **not recommended**.

What I take from the table:

- **The Pro gets 350 °C.** It was one of the seven upgrades promised in September, and it's in the
  sheet.
- **Closed-loop is Pro only.** The collision recovery the page presents for the whole "MX Series"
  isn't on the MX: its motors are open-loop.
- **The software is Windows only.** The sheet lists Windows as the only supported operating system: if
  you're on Mac or Linux, ask before you pledge.
- **Network: 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi only, no Ethernet**, on both.
- **Up to 1,850 W peak** for the Pro: that's the heated chamber. Worth keeping in mind on a domestic
  supply with other appliances running.
- **The 1.5GT belts aren't in the table**, but the campaign page now lists them among the series
  features ("1.5GT Ultra-Fine Timing Belts"). The page also describes **closed-loop motors** for the
  whole "MX Series", while the table puts them on the Pro only: if you're after the MX, ask in the
  comments before you decide.

## What's still to be confirmed

**The machines shown so far are prototypes.** The campaign page puts it in writing: specs, appearance,
packaging and firmware "may be optimized or updated during the production process", and the delivered
version prevails. In the campaign comments a user posted a reply attributed to InfiMech that goes
further. It's second-hand, so I'm reporting it as such:

- the units in the test videos are **pre-production prototypes**;
- for production the MX Pro would move to an **SSD2355 board with 512 MB of RAM**, replacing the X2600
  processor, and to **injection-moulded plastic parts** instead of 3D-printed ones;
- the **layer inconsistencies** seen in some prints have been passed to the engineers, with no cause
  given yet;
- **mixing nozzle diameters in one print isn't supported**, and it's not confirmed it will be at
  delivery;
- **tool change: 12–15 seconds target**, heating from 30 to 200 °C in 8 seconds;
- Klipper configuration, slicer profiles and tool-change macros will be **open source**.

Still open:

- **the cause of the layer lines**, and whether mixed nozzles will come;
- **the final specs**, to compare against the machine that arrives;
- **final shipping prices**, confirmed in the post-campaign survey.

One detail that inspires less confidence: the campaign's "Risks and challenges" section talks about
**"APS"**, with 18 months of development and 5 prototypes. APS isn't the MX. It looks like text
recycled from another campaign, and in the section meant to cover the risks of *this* project, that's
a slip worth pointing out.

## Where I stand

What I said last time still holds. The **MX at $299** ($359–379 delivered, $50 less once the
cashback comes back) is the cheapest toolchanger ever offered: if you can afford to see that money
arrive late, the balance between risk and upside is in your favour. For the **Pro**, reserve if you
like, but decide before the close, with answers to the open questions above.
