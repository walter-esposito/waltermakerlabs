This time there's a date: **the Kickstarter for the InfiMech MX series opens on 10 November 2026**.
It's on InfiMech's official homepage ("Launching on Kickstarter Nov 10"), with a button that leads to
the campaign's pre-launch page. I checked it on 5 November.

It's the fourth announced window. The first three (June, late September, late October) all slipped,
and I covered the third delay in
[InfiMech delays again: 7 free upgrades](../../3d-printing/infimech-delay-seven-free-upgrades/). The
difference is that this time it isn't a window, it's a specific day.

## The prices, and what you actually pay

| | MX (6 toolheads) | MX Pro (8 toolheads) |
|---|---|---|
| Super Early Bird at launch | $299 | $549 |
| With the $5 VIP deposit | $249 | $499 |
| List price | $399 | $1,399 |

The $249 and $499 figures that have been around since August **aren't a price you see at checkout**.
The $5 VIP deposit buys a **$50 credit**, paid back as cashback **after the campaign closes**. So on
10 November you pay $299 or $549, and the $50 comes back later.

And there's a condition that matters more than the deposit: **Super Early Bird slots are limited**,
and InfiMech hasn't said how many there are. It allocates them based on VIP reservations and
production capacity. If you're late, you don't pay $299 or $549: you pay the next tier, which hasn't
been published yet.

InfiMech doesn't publish prices in euros or pounds: exchange rate, shipping and VAT will only show up
on the campaign page.

**Launch time:** not published yet. VIP deposit holders get an alert shortly before launch, ahead of
the public one.

## The calendar problem: Formnext comes after

Formnext, the Frankfurt 3D printing trade show, runs **from 17 to 20 November**. InfiMech has
confirmed the MX Pro will be there.

In my piece on the delay I called Formnext the objective test: if the Pro prints in front of people,
the explanation for the delays holds up. But the calendar puts things in the wrong order. **Super
Early Bird slots are claimed on 10 November. The Pro is seen running from the 17th.** If you wait for
Formnext to decide, the best slots will very likely be gone.

### How to have both

On Kickstarter **you don't pay when you pledge**: your card is charged only when the campaign closes,
and until then you can **lower or cancel** your pledge. The one limit is the final 24 hours: then
Kickstarter won't let you cancel if pulling out would drop the project below its goal.

So if you're seriously interested in the MX Pro:

1. **On 10 November**, claim a Super Early Bird slot.
2. **From 17 to 20 November**, watch what happens at Formnext: whether the Pro actually prints, and how.
3. **Before the campaign closes**, decide. If the Pro isn't in Frankfurt, or it's there but switched
   off, cancel the pledge and you've paid nothing.

There's one condition: the campaign has to run **past 20 November**. You'll only see its length on the
10th, on the campaign page. Hardware campaigns usually stay open for weeks, but check: if it closed
before Formnext, this plan no longer works.

## What's still to be confirmed

- **The 1.5GT belts.** They were the only one of the seven upgrades still "under evaluation", and the
  reason for the last delay. The campaign page will show whether they made it.
- **The final specs.** InfiMech promised no downgrades and no extra cost for the upgrades. On
  10 November we'll see whether the campaign's spec sheet keeps that promise.
- **Delivery dates.** The number a crowdfunding campaign changes most often.
- **The price of extra toolheads**, never announced so far.

I'll update this article on 10 November with what's actually on the page.

## Where I stand

What I said last time still holds. The **MX at $299** ($249 after cashback) is the cheapest
toolchanger ever offered: if you can afford to see that money arrive late, the balance between risk
and upside is in your favour. For the **Pro**, it's the plan above: take the slot, decide after
Formnext.

<p class="avviso-affiliato">
<strong>A word on crowdfunding:</strong> backing a campaign means funding a product into existence,
not buying it. Pledges are charged when the campaign closes regardless of progress, and recovering
money from failed projects is rare. Only commit amounts you're comfortable seeing arrive late, or
not arrive at all.
</p>
