This time there's a date: **the Kickstarter for the InfiMech MX series opens on 10 November 2026**.
It's on InfiMech's official homepage ("Launching on Kickstarter Nov 10"), with a button that leads to
the campaign's pre-launch page. I checked it on 5 November.

It's the fourth announced window. The first three (June, late September, late October) all slipped,
and I covered the third delay in
[InfiMech delays again: 7 free upgrades](../../3d-printing/infimech-delay-seven-free-upgrades/). The
difference is that this time it isn't a window, it's a specific day.

> **Update, 8 November:** the campaign's pre-launch page now shows the warranty, the delivery
> roadmap, the add-ons, the full spec sheet for both machines and the eSUN partnership. You'll find them in the sections below. The video
> covers them too (in Italian, with chapters).

## The prices, and what you actually pay

| | MX (6 toolheads) | MX Pro (8 toolheads) |
|---|---|---|
| Super Early Bird at launch | $299 | $549 |
| With the $5 VIP deposit | $249 | $499 |
| List price | $399 | $1,399 |

The campaign page puts it this way: VIP "Pay $5 for VIP Access, Get $50 Cashback", Super Early Bird
"No deposit required". So you can claim a Super Early Bird slot without a deposit.

The $249 and $499 figures that have been around since August **aren't a price you see at checkout**.
The $5 VIP deposit buys a **$50 credit**, paid back as cashback **after the campaign closes**. So on
10 November you pay $299 or $549, and the $50 comes back later.

And there's a condition that matters more than the deposit: **Super Early Bird slots are limited**,
and InfiMech hasn't said how many there are. It allocates them based on VIP reservations and
production capacity. If you're late, you don't pay $299 or $549: you pay the next tier, which hasn't
been published yet.

InfiMech doesn't publish prices in euros or pounds: exchange rate, shipping and VAT will only show up
on the campaign page.

**Launch time:** InfiMech sends it by email to VIP deposit holders, shortly before launch and ahead of
the public alert.

## The calendar problem: Formnext comes after

Formnext, the Frankfurt 3D printing trade show, runs **from 17 to 20 November**. InfiMech has
confirmed the MX Pro will be there.

In my piece on the delay I called Formnext the objective test: if the Pro prints in front of people,
the explanation for the delays holds up. But the calendar puts things in the wrong order. **Super
Early Bird slots are claimed on 10 November. The Pro is seen running from the 17th.** If you wait for
Formnext to decide, the best slots will very likely be gone.

### How to have both

On Kickstarter **you don't pay when you pledge**: your card is charged only when the campaign closes,
and until then you can **lower or cancel** your pledge. The one limit is the final 24 hours: then
Kickstarter won't let you cancel if pulling out would drop the project below its goal.

So if you're seriously interested in the MX Pro:

1. **On 10 November**, claim a Super Early Bird slot.
2. **From 17 to 20 November**, watch what happens at Formnext: whether the Pro actually prints, and how.
3. **Before the campaign closes**, decide. If the Pro isn't in Frankfurt, or it's there but switched
   off, cancel the pledge and you've paid nothing.

There's one condition: the campaign has to run **past 20 November**. You'll only see its length on the
10th, on the campaign page. Hardware campaigns usually stay open for weeks, but check: if it closed
before Formnext, this plan no longer works.

## The roadmap: deliveries from Q1 2027

The campaign page lays out the project's history and the next milestones:

| When | Milestone |
|---|---|
| July 2024 | Project kickoff (codename F04) |
| November 2024 | First prototype, 10 units |
| May 2025 | Pilot run V2.0, 20 units |
| September 2025 | Official names: MX and MX Pro |
| December 2025 | Public debut at the Dubai exhibition, 50 units in testing |
| May 2026 | Global beta with creators |
| September 2026 | Pilot production, 200 units |
| **November 2026** | **Kickstarter** |
| **December 2026** | **Mass production** |
| **Q1 2027** | **First batch shipped to backers** |

Two things to read carefully. Mass production is planned **one month after** the campaign opens:
that's a tight schedule for a project that has already moved its launch three times. And "first
batch" doesn't mean every backer: if you join later in the campaign, your machine may come later.

## Warranty and support

| Coverage | Length |
|---|---|
| Structural, mechanical and electronic components | 12 months, **2 years where legally required (EU and UK)** |
| VIP reservation holders | 2-year full warranty on core components |
| Wear parts (hotend assembly, extruder gear, PTFE tube) | 3 months |

The page also lists a "6-month limited warranty" without saying what it covers: worth asking InfiMech
during the campaign.

What matters most is the **service method**: "Official replacement parts shipped directly to users",
meaning **they send you the part and you fit it**. There's no repair centre to send the printer to.
On a toolchanger with six or eight heads, that means sooner or later you'll be taking a head apart
yourself.

## The add-ons

During the campaign you'll be able to add to your pledge: **spare toolhead, build plate, feeder
module, filament, 10 W laser module, camera and side-blower fan**.

Prices aren't published yet. The spec sheet below does make clear who they're for:

- the **camera** and the **side-blower fan** are **standard on the MX Pro** and **optional on the MX**;
- the **laser module** is **optional on the MX Pro** and **not available on the MX**.

## eSUN and the giveaways

InfiMech has announced a partnership with **eSUN**, one of the most widespread filament brands, with
InfiMech × eSUN co-branded filaments. During the campaign they promise giveaways and daily rewards:
**eSUN filaments and eSUN discount coupons**. Rules, schedule and how to enter will be published at
launch.

## Full spec sheet: MX vs MX Pro

This is the table published on the campaign page. InfiMech calls it **provisional and subject to
change** until the machine ships.

| | MX Pro | MX |
|---|---|---|
| External dimensions | 453 × 488 × 563 mm | 361 × 401 × 402 mm |
| Build volume | 300 × 300 × 300 mm | 220 × 220 × 220 mm |
| Weight | 26 kg | 13 kg |
| Frame | Steel, enclosed | Steel, open |
| Toolheads | 8 | 6 |
| Max nozzle temperature | 350 °C | 310 °C |
| Motors | **Closed-loop** | Open-loop |
| Max speed | 800 mm/s | 600 mm/s |
| Max acceleration | 20,000 mm/s² | 20,000 mm/s² |
| Bed | 110 °C, textured plate | 110 °C, textured plate |
| Heated chamber | 60–65 °C | No |
| Air filter | HEPA and activated carbon | No |
| Camera | 1080p 30 fps, standard | Optional |
| AI monitoring | Yes | No |
| Side-blower fan | Standard | Optional |
| Laser module | Optional | Not available |
| Max power | 1,850 W at 220 V (1,300 W at 110 V) | 1,150 W at 220 V (700 W at 110 V) |

**Shared:** HRC50+ hardened steel nozzles, induction heating, wireless thermistor, 1.75 mm filament,
adaptive calibration, blob, run-out and tangle detection, power-loss recovery, plate detection, 5"
screen (480 × 854), 8 GB storage plus microSD, OrcaSlicer or InfiMech Orca, operating temperature
10–30 °C.

**Materials.** The Pro prints PLA, PETG, ABS, ASA, TPU, PET, PA and PC, plus carbon- or glass-filled
grades (PLA, PETG, ABS, ASA, PA, PA6, PA12, PAHT, PPA, PET). The MX is meant for PLA, PETG, TPU, PVA,
PLA-CF and PETG-CF; ABS, ASA, PA, PC and PET are **not recommended**.

What I take from the table:

- **The Pro gets 350 °C.** It was one of the seven upgrades promised in September, and it's in the
  sheet.
- **Closed-loop is Pro only.** The collision recovery the page presents for the whole "MX Series"
  isn't on the MX: its motors are open-loop.
- **The software is Windows only.** The sheet lists Windows as the only supported operating system: if
  you're on Mac or Linux, ask before you pledge.
- **Network: 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi only, no Ethernet**, on both.
- **Up to 1,850 W peak** for the Pro: that's the heated chamber. Worth keeping in mind on a domestic
  supply with other appliances running.
- **The 1.5GT belts aren't in the sheet.**

## What's still to be confirmed

- **The 1.5GT belts.** They were the only one of the seven upgrades still "under evaluation", and the
  reason for the last delay. Neither the pre-launch page nor the spec sheet mentions them.
- **The final specs.** The sheet is declared provisional: it's the one to compare against the machine
  that arrives.
- **Add-on prices**, including the spare toolhead.
- **The campaign length**, which decides whether waiting for Formnext works.

I'll update this article on 10 November with what's actually on the page.

## Where I stand

What I said last time still holds. The **MX at $299** ($249 after cashback) is the cheapest
toolchanger ever offered: if you can afford to see that money arrive late, the balance between risk
and upside is in your favour. For the **Pro**, it's the plan above: take the slot, decide after
Formnext.

<p class="avviso-affiliato">
<strong>A word on crowdfunding:</strong> backing a campaign means funding a product into existence,
not buying it. Pledges are charged when the campaign closes regardless of progress, and recovering
money from failed projects is rare. Only commit amounts you're comfortable seeing arrive late, or
not arrive at all.
</p>
