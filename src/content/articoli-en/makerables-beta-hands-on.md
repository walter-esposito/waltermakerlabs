I've got into the **Makerables beta**, Makera's platform aiming to change how people approach CNC. This is a genuine first contact: I open it and explore it for the first time.

> This isn't a review assembled after the fact. It's first use, with the doubts that come with it.

## The problem it's trying to solve

To see why a platform like this makes sense, you have to look at where desktop CNC got stuck.

3D printing went mainstream when **slicers** hid the complexity: load a model, pick a profile, hit print. The software translates your intent into thousands of movements without you needing to know how.

In CNC, that step never really happened. CAM — the software that turns a model into toolpaths — still asks you to decide machining strategy, depth of cut, feed rate, spindle speed and cutter type. Getting a parameter wrong doesn't produce an ugly part: it breaks the cutter, or the workpiece, or both.

That's the barrier keeping most makers out. A platform that lowers it would do for CNC what slicers did for 3D printing.

## The doubt worth holding onto

Every layer of abstraction that simplifies also removes control. With slicers the trade-off worked because a failed print costs a few hours and some filament. In CNC a wrong choice costs a cutter, a block of aluminium or a spindle.

So the real question about Makerables isn't whether it simplifies — it's **how conservative it is when it isn't sure**. A system that slows down and lightens the pass when in doubt is useful. One that guesses on the optimistic side, much less so.
