On **19 September** InfiMech pushed back the Kickstarter for the MX series. It is the third announced window and the third slip: the target is now **late October, early November**.

Along with the delay, though, they did something almost nobody does: they published the list of changes to the machine and committed to them being **free for backers**.

They are worth reading one at a time, because they are not equivalent — and one in particular is not confirmed at all.

## The seven upgrades, and what they actually change

**1. 8 mm die-cast aluminium bed** *(MX and MX Pro)*

The most substantial of the seven. A thin bed deforms as it heats, and the software compensation ends up chasing a moving target: the result is inconsistent first layers with no obvious cause. Eight millimetres of die-cast aluminium is a high-end specification, not a $249 one.

**2. 1.5GT belts** *(MX and MX Pro)* — ⚠️ **this is the exception**

The current prototype runs 2GT belts, inherited from the previous generation. Community members suggested moving to 1.5GT, and InfiMech said they would **evaluate and test** that option.

Evaluate, not adopt. It is the only one of the seven that isn't a decision already made — and, by their own admission, it is the one that caused the delay: touching the motion system means redoing most of the validation they had already completed.

**3. 24V expansion port on the mainboard** *(MX and MX Pro)*

Lets you connect accessories without cutting into the board. For anyone who modifies their own machines, this is the kind of detail that adds years to a printer's useful life.

**4. Hotend up to 350 °C** *(MX Pro only)*

Enables PC, PA and fibre-filled materials. It only makes sense on the Pro because that's the one with the 65 °C heated chamber: without it, 350 degrees at the nozzle doesn't help, because the part shrinks as it cools too quickly.

This is why the difference between MX and MX Pro isn't "bigger" — it's **different materials**.

**5. Revised nozzle wiping structure** *(MX and MX Pro)*

On a toolchanger, cleaning at the tool change is where defects are born: any residue left behind ends up in the next colour.

**6. Additional cooling fans on the toolhead** *(MX and MX Pro)*

They reduce the risk of clogging. On a machine with six or eight heads, a clog doesn't stop one head — it ruins the entire print.

**7. New toolhead PCB with onboard memory** *(MX and MX Pro)*

The head keeps its nozzle and filament parameters **even when powered down**. On a machine where heads get swapped, that's the difference between a system that knows what's fitted and one you have to tell every time.

---

> **Six of these seven are decisions already made. One is an evaluation — and it's the one that pushed the dates back.**

## The two promises that matter more than the seven

In the official group, a user lined up nine questions asking for point-by-point confirmation. The first seven map exactly onto the upgrades. The last two are the ones about **what you actually receive**, not what has been announced:

- do the upgrades stay at **no additional cost** for backers?
- will any already-announced features be **removed or reduced**?

InfiMech had already answered both in the 19 September post: no additional cost — *"Our answer is straightforward: No"* — and no spec reductions barring serious quality problems.

So the answers exist, in writing. What's missing is **re-confirmation after the changes**, which is a different and entirely legitimate request: a promise made before changing seven things isn't automatically valid afterwards.

## Why the MX Pro hasn't been seen running

It's the question doing the rounds, and InfiMech's answer has three parts:

1. MX and MX Pro share the same technical base: what works on one counts as validation for the other
2. There are far fewer Pro prototypes
3. A second prototype run started in late August, 50 units per model, but component delays slowed assembly: every available prototype is tied up in validation

**Technically this holds.** With a small prototype pool, pulling one off the bench to shoot a video genuinely slows development.

But it's also the most convenient explanation available, because it's impossible to verify from the outside.

### There is, however, a date that settles it

InfiMech confirmed the **MX Pro will be at Formnext**.

That's the objective test: if the machine is there and printing in front of people, the explanation holds. If it's absent, or present but switched off, then the problem is something else — and nobody's opinion will be needed to work that out.

## Is the scepticism legitimate?

In the group, someone brought up Phrozen and the ARCO printer, announced and delivered three years later. Another pointed out they were "on track" for the previous window too.

That shouldn't be dismissed. It should be separated out.

**What makes this case different**

- **The machine exists.** It printed live at the Japan RepRap Festival in May and runs in their Yiwu store. Nobody is funding a render.
- **They published the list of still-open bugs.** That's rare transparency, and a vapourware project wouldn't do it.

**What gives the sceptics a point**

- Three announced windows, three slips
- The MX Pro list price went from **$899 to $1,399** in July: the discount remains, but full price moved
- The $5 deposit **does not guarantee** the discounted price: you need a capped Super Early Bird slot

The delay itself isn't the problem. A delay with technically sensible reasons beats delivering an unvalidated product on time. The point is a different one: **every time it slips, something else in the terms changes too.**

## Who it makes sense for, today

**If you can afford to lose $250** — the MX at VIP pricing is the cheapest toolchanger ever proposed. If you can write off that amount, the balance of risk and upside is in your favour.

**If you want the Pro** — I'd wait for Formnext. Between $499 and a $1,399 list price the gap is enormous, but it's also the machine nobody has yet seen running in public.

**If you need to print now** — this isn't your purchase. Buy a machine that exists in a shop.
