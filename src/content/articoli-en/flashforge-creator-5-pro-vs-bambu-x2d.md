Multicolour FDM has always had the same problem: **wasted filament**. Every colour change needs a purge. With Bambu's AMS, with any filament-switching system, that's how it has always gone.

Flashforge took a different route with the Creator 5 Pro: **four independent toolheads**, a physical head change, zero contamination. No purge, because there's nothing to purge.

## How FlashSwap works, and why it differs from an AMS

The difference is architectural, not a matter of tuning.

In an AMS system there is one filament at a time inside a single nozzle. To change colour you have to push out all the previous material, otherwise the new colour comes out contaminated. That ejected material is pure waste, and it repeats at **every** change.

With four physical heads, each filament has its own dedicated path from start to finish. The change is mechanical: one head parks, another moves into position. There's no mixing, so there's nothing to eject.

It's the same principle as the Snapmaker U1, with a step up in equipment.

## The claimed numbers

- **84% less wasted filament**
- **5&times; faster** on multicolour

These are manufacturer figures and should be treated as such, but the order of magnitude is consistent with what you'd expect moving from a purge system to a head-change one.

## What the Pro version adds

- **Actively heated chamber up to 65 &deg;C.** Not passively enclosed: heated. That's the requirement for ABS, ASA and filled materials, which without a hot chamber warp and delaminate.
- **Dual filtration: medical-grade HEPA H13 plus activated carbon.** The carbon captures volatile organic compounds, the HEPA catches ultrafine particles. On an enclosed machine running ABS in a room people live in, that isn't an accessory.

## The comparison that matters

The Creator 5 Pro lines up against the Bambu Lab X2D with an aggressive launch price, and the comparison has to be made on the kind of multicolour work you print:

- **If you print a lot of multicolour**, the independent-head architecture is structurally better on waste and time. This isn't a software optimisation question.
- **If you print almost everything in a single colour**, all of this is complexity you pay for and don't use: four heads are four things that can fall out of alignment.
- **If you need multi-material** (not just multicolour), independent heads allow different temperatures per material, which a single-nozzle system cannot do by definition.
